Why labels weren't it

Three channel types, and nobody can find any of them

The model already has four types — Public, Private, DirectMessage, Team — but in practice only three get used. Everything that should be a team channel is a private channel someone happened to invite you to. That produces two failures at once.

What we haveWhat it means todayWhat breaks
Public A channel that includes people outside the company The name says "open to everyone," the behavior says the opposite
Private Invite-only group channel — where all real team channels live Undiscoverable by design. You can't join what you can't see
Direct message One-to-one or small group, created by messaging someone Fine — but it's stacked in the same list as the other two
Team Team-scoped, auto-joinable — already in the domain model Nothing creates them, so nothing surfaces them

A new hire lands in a handful of channels, has no list of what else exists, and has to be invited into every conversation by someone who remembers they exist. Labels made a flat list slightly tidier. They didn't make anything findable.

The shape

Topics group team channels. The sidebar splits in two.

  • Topics are team-owned and public to the team. Joining the team subscribes you to every topic; you opt out of the ones you don't care about rather than being invited into the ones you do.
  • Subscription and visibility are separate settings. Unsubscribing stops notifications. Hiding removes it from the sidebar. They're different needs and they get different controls.
  • Collapsing hides the quiet channels, not the loud ones. A collapsed topic still lists the channels with unreads or mentions — so collapsing is how you get a topic down to what's actually happening in it.
  • Smart topics match by rule. A channel can belong to several topics at once, and any channel in at least one topic stays out of Uncategorized.
  • Team channels live above the line; everything else below. Topics on top, then one section holding external, private, and direct-message channels — the conversations that belong to a specific set of people rather than to the company.

Message pane is a stub — the sidebar is the subject.

1 Topic menu

Two sections, because they're two different decisions. Notifications sets the level channels inherit. Actions holds the two ways out: hide it from your sidebar but stay in, or unsubscribe and leave the topic's channels entirely.

Try: hover Engineering → ⋯ → set Mentions only.

2 Hidden, not unsubscribed

Hiding is per-person and reversible from the same place every time — the Hidden pill. A hidden topic you're still subscribed to shows a dot on the pill when it has mentions, so nothing disappears silently.

Try: toggle Hidden to reveal hidden topics inline.

3 Creation

The + menu asks the question that matters first: is this for the team, for a specific group, or for someone outside. A team channel picks a topic at creation, which is what keeps Uncategorized small.

Try: + → New team channel.

4 Drag and drop

Drag a channel onto a topic to move it; hold ⌥ to leave it in both. Drag a topic header to reorder. Dragging a private channel up into a topic is one route to converting it — it asks first, because it makes the channel team-visible.

Try: drag #perf-notes from Uncategorized into Engineering.

5 Your order, not the team's

The ≡ button on the Topics header sets how they're stacked: Custom, A–Z, Recent activity, or Unread first. Order is stored per person, so arranging your sidebar never moves anyone else's. Dragging or using Move up switches you to custom and keeps what was already on screen.

Try: ≡ → Recent activity, then drag a topic.

6 Promoting a private channel

Every private and external channel carries a Make a team channel button — in its header and on the row itself. It asks which topic to file it under and says plainly what changes: the team can find it and join without an invite, and the history comes along.

Try: open #founders and use the header button.

7 Discovery

Browse topics is the answer to "what exists here." Every topic, every channel in it, joinable without an invite — the screen a new teammate gets pointed at instead of a list of names to ask.

Try: Browse topics at the bottom of the sidebar.

8 Keyboard path

Drag and drop is the fast path, never the only one. Channels move via Move to topic in the ⋯ menu; topics reorder via Move up and Move down in theirs. Anything you can drag, you can also do from a menu.

Try: hover any channel → ⋯ → Move to topic.

The rule behind the menu

Subscribed and visible are independent

Collapsing these into one control is the mistake every version of this feature makes. "I don't want to hear about it" and "I don't want to look at it" are different sentences, and people mean both separately.

NotificationsShown in sidebarHidden
All activity Default for everyone on the team. Unreads and mentions both count. Not in the list; mentions still reach Activity and carry a count on the Hidden pill.
Mentions only In the list, no unread weight — only direct mentions raise an orange @ count. An archive you can still be pulled into by name.
Nothing Visible because you want to glance at it, silent because you don't work on it. Fully out of the way, still one click back from the Hidden pill.

Unsubscribe is the third, coarser action, and it's why the topic menu has an Actions section rather than one more radio button: it removes you from the topic altogether — its channels leave your sidebar — and you rejoin from Browse topics. Hiding is a view preference; unsubscribing is membership.

Data model

The channel model doesn't have to change

ChannelType::Team already exists in crates/channels/src/domain/models.rs, along with team_id, auto_join_team, and convert_to_team_channel in the channel service. The type isn't broken — it's unused, because nothing in the product creates a team channel or shows you one you haven't joined. Topics give it a reason to exist.

PieceStatusNotes
comms_channels.channel_type unchanged Team channels sit under topics; public / private / DM sit below the line. No new variant, no backfill of existing rows.
channel_topics new Team-scoped: id, team_id, name, description, sort order, created_by.
channel_topic_channels new Many-to-many. A channel in ≥1 topic never appears in Uncategorized.
channel_topic_rules new Smart topics: name pattern, creator, participants, or activity window. Evaluated on membership read and on channel create/rename.
user_channel_topic_prefs new Per user per topic: notification level, hidden, collapsed, manual sort position. Absent row = subscribed, visible, default order — which is what makes "subscribed by default" free.
Channel → team conversion exists convert_to_team_channel already handles the private → team transition the drag-up gesture needs.

One thing genuinely changes behaviorally: team channels become listable to people who aren't participants. That's a read-authorization change in the channel list service, not a schema change — and it's the change the whole feature is actually about.

Still open

Decisions this concept doesn't make for you

  • Migration. Existing private channels that are obviously team channels need a path up. A one-time admin screen that proposes conversions per channel, or leave them and let topics fill in going forward.
  • Does joining a topic join its channels? Subscribing to a topic could mean "show me these channels" without adding you as a participant — cheaper, but then unread state and posting rights diverge from membership.
  • Who creates topics. Anyone on the team, or admins only. Anyone is the right default for a company this size and the wrong one at 200 people.
  • Smart topic authoring. The rule builder is a real surface and isn't drawn here — the concept only shows what a smart topic looks like once it exists.
