Macro agents live and work inside your workspace, so one prompt can reach your email, tasks, docs, calls, channels, and connected tools. They can search and read anything you can see, draft emails and documents, create and update tasks, post in channels, and run on a schedule. Everything they do is attributed to you and bounded by your permissions.

### The Agents workspace

Agents lists every conversation you've had, named automatically from your first message, and you can pick any of them back up where you left off. Ask for summaries, clarifications, drafts, or a status check. Agents answer using everything in your workspace, not just the current thread.

> Use shortcut <kbd>g</kbd> \+ <kbd>a</kbd> to go to Agents.

**Chat** and **Code** are the two kinds of conversation.

- **Chat** works in your workspace: documents, email, tasks, channels, and the rest of what you can see.
- **Code** works on a repository. The session shows the repository, branch, and linked pull request. See [GitHub integration](/integrations/github).

The composer starts in **Auto**, which sends each prompt to your usual chat agent, or to your usual coding agent when the work is code. Choose **Chat** or **Code** to start that kind every time.

Group conversations by **Type** and the sidebar splits into **Chat** and **Code**. Filter by **Type** to show one of them.

**New conversation** starts a conversation. The **Agent** selector in the composer sets the agent or model. **Search conversations** filters by title. Right-click a conversation to rename, favorite, share, or delete it.

A notification appears on [Home](/product/home) when an agent finishes or needs an answer.

### Full workspace context

Agents answer from your email, messages, tasks, docs, calls, and connected tools, not just your chat history. That's why you can ask a broad question ("what's the latest on the Acme deal?") without pointing them at anything.

When you want to be exact, @mention a specific channel, doc, task, or thread right in your prompt. That pins the context and skips the search, so agents work from precisely the item you mean. See [Mentions in Agents](/concepts/mentions#mentions-in-agents).

<Note>
  Agents inherit your permissions: they can only see what you can see, and anything they create or send is attributed to you. Start prompts with "draft, don't send" until you're comfortable letting them act.
</Note>

### What agents can do

Agents have a broad tool set and will reach for the right tools on their own. In one conversation they can:

- **Search & read** — find and read across docs, threads, chats, and records
- **Write docs** — create, edit, and rename documents
- **Tasks & CRM** — create tasks and set status, priority, assignees, and other properties on tasks and company records
- **Email** — draft a reply in an editable composer for you to review and send
- **Channels** — read a channel or thread and post messages back into it
- **Calls** — read call recordings and transcripts and turn them into tasks or summaries
- **Web** — search the web and fetch pages when the answer isn't in your workspace
- **Import** — pull content in from Notion, Linear, and Slack when you migrate

### Agents in channels

Mention **@Macro** in any [channel](/product/channels) to pull agents into the conversation with the whole channel as context. They show up in the normal @-mention typeahead, read the thread, and reply inline. Agents can also post their results back into a channel or a specific thread as part of a longer task.

<Note>
  **@Macro** invokes the built-in agents. They're separate from **custom bots** — named integrations you create with their own API tokens and webhooks to post into channels programmatically.
</Note>

### Routines

A [routine](/product/routines) is a saved prompt that runs on a schedule or when activity happens in Macro, such as a new email or a task status change.

### Skills

A [skill](/product/skills) is a saved set of instructions an agent follows. Type `/` in an agent composer to attach one.

### Custom agents

**Settings → Agents** creates agents with a name, instructions, default model, and the machine it runs on: Macro's own, a hosted coding agent, or your own computer. See [Bring your own agent](/AI/bring-your-own) to connect Claude Code, Codex, or another local agent. See [Cursor](/AI/cursor) to run coding agents on your Cursor plan.

### Choosing a model

A model picker sits in the composer. You can choose a model per conversation depending on how difficult you deem the question. Your choice is remembered for that conversation and carried forward when you start a new one.

On the Free plan, the model is Gemini 3.8 Flash. Pro and Max include Anthropic's Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5, and Haiku 4.5, OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra and GPT-5.6, and open-weight models.

Agent chats count as AI usage. Each plan includes a monthly amount, and some models use more of it than others. **Settings → Usage** shows how much of that amount you have used. See [Pricing](/account/billing).

### MCP setup

Macro's MCP server lets AI clients like Claude Code, Codex, and Cursor work with your workspace directly. Connect once by going to **Settings → MCP server** (authorize with your Macro account — MCP access is included on every plan, Free included), or point a client at the endpoint below.

```text
https://mcp-server.macro.com/mcp
```

See [MCP setup](/AI/mcp/overview) for client-by-client instructions.

### @mentioning

Agent conversations are entities like everything else in Macro, so you can @mention a conversation from a doc, task, email, or channel to link back to a conversation. And it works the other way too: @mention docs, tasks, channels, people, and more inside a prompt to hand agents exactly the context you want.
