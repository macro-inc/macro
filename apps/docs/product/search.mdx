Search is the easiest way to find specific files in Macro. When using search for multiple terms, use quotations for an exact match. Otherwise, the default is to independently match terms in a document.

Put a minus before a word or a quoted phrase to leave out the results that contain it: `budget -draft` finds items about the budget that don't mention "draft", and `-"team lunch"` leaves out the exact phrase. A minus inside a word, as in `e-mail`, is part of the word. A minus word needs another word beside it, except in a search of email alone.

> Use the <kbd>/</kbd> shortcut to open up search or the <kbd>cmd</kbd>+<kbd>k</kbd> menu to search by name.

Type [search operators](#search-operators) such as `type:task` or `tag:finance` to narrow results.

To further narrow search results, combine your keywords with an @mention of a specific person.

Search covers more than titles: document bodies, email threads, channel messages, call transcripts, and file contents are all indexed. You can also ask an [agent](/product/agents) to find things for you; agents use the same index.

## Search operators

The Search field reads search operators: a name, a colon, and a value that narrows the search. Combine as many as you like with ordinary words, in any order. For example, `launch type:task status:in_progress assignee:me` finds your tasks in progress that mention "launch". Operators on their own list everything they match, most recently updated first.

As you type, the field suggests operators, their values, people, tags, and channels. On a computer, a word such as `tasks` or `unread`, or the start of a person's name, also offers the operator it stands for. Hover the info button beside the **Search** title for a few examples; on a phone, tap it in the empty search field. On a phone, the **From**, **Type**, **Tag**, **Date**, and **Unread** buttons above the results start an operator for you.

| Operator | Finds |
| --- | --- |
| `type:task` | One kind of item: `task`, `doc`, `email`, `channel`, `call`, `folder`, `agent`, or `calendar` |
| `status:in_progress` | Tasks with a status: `not_started`, `in_progress`, `in_review`, `completed`, or `canceled`. Calls that were `missed`, `attended`, or `unattended` |
| `priority:high` | Tasks with a priority: `urgent`, `high`, `medium`, or `low` |
| `assignee:me` | Tasks assigned to you, or to someone by address, such as `assignee:ana@example.com` |
| `author:me` | Tasks you created, or that someone created, by address |
| `tag:finance` | Items with the tag Finance |
| `inbox:ana@example.com` | Email in one of your connected inboxes |
| `in:#launch` | Messages and calls in a channel you're in |
| `from:ana` | Mail from Ana, as in [Search mail](#search-mail). With `type:channel` or `type:call`, who sent the message or spoke: `from:me` or an address |
| `"q3 plan"` | Containing the exact phrase "q3 plan" |

The operators in [Search mail](#search-mail), such as `subject:lunch`, `is:unread`, `has:attachment`, and `newer_than:7d`, work here too, and keep the results to email.

A few details:

- Put `OR` between two values of the same operator to match either one: `tag:finance OR tag:legal`, `status:completed OR status:canceled`. Without `OR`, every operator must match, so `tag:finance tag:legal` finds items with both tags.
- `assignee:` and `author:` take `me` or a full address, and `inbox:` takes the address of one of your inboxes. A bare name such as `assignee:ana` is left out of the search until you pick someone from the suggestions or type their full address.
- A minus before a word, such as `-draft`, leaves it out only when the search is limited to email, by `type:email` or an operator from Search mail. Elsewhere the word is searched as usual.
- Quote a value that has a space in it: `tag:"team offsite"`, `in:#"team lunch"`.
- Each operator keeps the results to the kinds of item it applies to. `status:` and `priority:` find tasks (or calls, for a call status), `tag:` finds items that can have tags, and `in:#` finds channels and calls.
- A sender on its own, such as `type:channel from:me`, needs a word beside it.
- `@ana` finds items that mention Ana. `from:ana` finds what Ana sent.
- Operator names are not case sensitive, so `Type:Task` works too.
- An operator with a value it can't read, such as `type:tasks` or `priority:critical`, is searched as ordinary words.

## Search mail

Macro Mail's search field reads search operators, as Gmail does: a name, a colon, and a value that narrows the search. Combine as many as you like with ordinary words, in any order. For example, `budget from:ana has:attachment newer_than:30d` finds mail from Ana with an attachment, sent in the last 30 days, that mentions "budget".

| Operator | Finds mail |
| --- | --- |
| `from:ana` | From Ana, by the sender's name, or by a full address such as `from:ana@example.com` |
| `to:ben` | Sent to Ben, in To, Cc or Bcc |
| `cc:ben`, `bcc:ben` | With Ben in the Cc, or in the Bcc |
| `subject:lunch` | With "lunch" in the subject |
| `"team lunch"` | Containing the exact phrase "team lunch" |
| `subject:"team lunch"` | Whose subject contains the exact phrase |
| `label:travel` | With the mailbox label or Macro tag Travel |
| `in:sent` | In a view: `inbox`, `signal`, `noise`, `favorites`, `sent`, `drafts`, `calendar`, `shared` or `archived` |
| `in:travel` | With the label or tag Travel, when no view has that name |
| `is:unread`, `is:read` | Unread, or read |
| `is:starred` | Starred |
| `is:shared` | In threads shared with you |
| `has:attachment` | With an attachment |
| `after:2026/06/01` | Sent on or after a date |
| `before:2026/07/01` | Sent before a date |
| `newer_than:7d` | Sent in the last 7 days |
| `older_than:1m` | Sent more than a month ago |

Put a minus before a word or an operator to leave matching mail out: `-newsletter` skips mail containing "newsletter", and `-from:ana` skips mail from Ana. Every view but Signal and Noise can be left out with `-in:`, such as `-in:archived`.

A few details:

- Words match from their start: `subject:lunch` also finds "lunchtime", and `from:ana` finds Ana and Anabel but not Diana. A full address matches only that address.
- Quote a value that has a space in it: `subject:"team lunch"`, `label:"Q3 planning"`.
- Write dates as `2026/06/01` or `2026-06-01`. They are read in your time zone.
- `newer_than:` and `older_than:` take a number and a unit: `d` for days, `w` for weeks, `m` for months, `y` for years.
- Operator names are not case sensitive, so `From:ana` works too.
- An operator with a value it can't read, such as `is:important`, is searched as an ordinary word.
