Channels end-to-end suite: main against approach B

119 browser tests of what people see in channels, run unchanged on main, on main built with the GraphQL sources flag, and on approach B (channels on a local message index). 15 November 2026. Pull request #10864, CI gate green on its last commit, never to be merged.

B does not pass. The suite cannot give proof that B causes no channel regressions, because it found three that main does not have, with or without the flag:

  1. A message sent in the first moments after a channel opens is lost if the page is reloaded within about half a second. 9 of 10 whole-suite runs.
  2. A person's own messages, sent quickly, show out of the order they were sent. 8 of 10 runs.
  3. Removing a reaction shortly after a reload shows as removed on that page but is never sent. 6 of 8 when repeated alone.

No whole-suite run on B was clean: 10 runs, 1 to 4 failed tests each.

How each test was shown able to fail

For each test the product was broken on purpose where the behaviour lives, the app rebuilt, and the test watched failing. The 56 breaks, what every test did under each, and the error each test failed with are kept here; they were taken out of the committed suite on 17 November 2026. Since 17 November 2026 main has one build of the app (the GraphQL sources flag was removed), so the suite now runs once in CI and its ledgers list 8 known defects and 15 intermittent ones; the comparison on this page is of main as it was before that. 108 tests have such a proof. The other 11 assert behaviour main lacks today, so they fail on main unbroken; they are marked expected-to-fail and turn the run red when the defect is fixed.

Limits of these proofs:

  • The five reconnect and two-window tests, and CMP-03 and CMP-13, are proven only by a general break (the channel's messages never load, or live events are ignored). That shows they can fail, not that they fail for the exact behaviour in their title. CMP-13 has since failed on B for its own behaviour.
  • DM-04 does not notice a group losing its name; a break of that failed nothing.
  • DM-05's title says nothing can be sent until you type; it checks only that Send is off while empty.
  • Eleven tests have one check that a known, intermittent defect of main fails in some runs (below). That check is recorded when it fails but cannot fail the run. On B those checks are just as silent: ACC-06, CHN-02 and UNR-04 showed on B in most runs without turning it red.
  • The proofs were made before the last changes to CHN-02, ACC-06, CHN-08, UNR-08 and SCROLL-04; the breaks were not run again against the changed tests.

What the suite does not prove

  • Nothing about the 13 uncovered behaviours, including calls, agents, phones and notifications. B could break any of them unseen.
  • Nothing at scale. The largest channel has 230 messages; a local index is the kind of change whose problems show with large histories and many channels.
  • Chromium on desktop only. Not Safari, not the desktop or mobile apps, whose storage differs.
  • B was tested 118 commits behind main, on one machine, never on CI's stack. Three of main's defects turned out to depend on the machine; B's results may too.
  • Fresh accounts. Upgrade from a device's existing stored data to B's index was not exercised.
  • Timing-dependent bugs are sampled, not excluded. Two of B's three regressions depend on acting within a second or so of a page load; others of that kind may exist that no test happens to hit.
  • Main is not perfectly steady either: one unexplained failure in 19 flag-off runs, one in 13 flag-on runs.

In CI

Channels end-to-end runs both builds on every PR that touches channel code, the suite or its stack, and is part of CI gate. Each job builds the stack, seeds six casts of four people, runs the suite and then checks the run against the ledger; reports and traces are kept as artifacts. Two things had to change for CI: document storage needs a larger worker stack when built without optimization (RUST_MIN_STACK), and the three machine-dependent defects above.
